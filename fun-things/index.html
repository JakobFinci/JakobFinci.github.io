Not everything I like having done belongs on a CV. Some things are just too strange, too specific, or too funny to squeeze into a bullet point. I wanted somewhere to put a few of those things, so here we are.

A small footnote in All Tomorrows

I've long been fascinated by evolutionary biology, particularly the strange and often unexpected directions life can take under different environmental and selective pressures. That fascination made C. M. Kösemen's All Tomorrows, a work of speculative evolution imagining the distant evolutionary futures of humanity, especially appealing to me. I backed the print edition of the book, and my name even appears on Kösemen's thank-you page. It's an acknowledgment for supporting the project rather than contributing to it, but having a small place in the history of a book that combines two of my favorite interests, evolutionary biology and science fiction, is something I'm quite happy about.

The Bee Movie incident

I participated in MSCHF's project to recreate Bee Movie through individually redrawn, crowdsourced frames. Over 29,000 people participated, myself included, and the movie was finished within a few hours. I ended up drawing so many frames, I made it onto the first page of animation credits, drawing the 146th most frames, or around 100 frames. While this is a very unorthodox achievement, I am very proud to have been a part of this impressive statement on the absurdity of modern copyright and think it is worth sharing! Better yet, you can watch the actual finished project here.

The distinguished office of Di-Er Teamaster

At Babson I was a "Di-Er Teamaster" in the Tea Society, which is a real title I once signed things with. We organized tea events, brewed for fellow students, and occasionally pulled from my own tea collection. Tea has always been one of those subjects I can get unreasonably interested in, so this was a pretty good excuse to share it with other people.

I took the interest a little further, too. On November 5, 2024, I completed the Tea Sommelier Certification Course and earned the designation of ITMA Certified Tea Sommelier. It's not an academic degree, obviously, but it's probably the closest thing I'll ever have to a tea degree.

Letters to a Pre-Scientist

I was selected to participate as a STEM pen pal with Letters to a Pre-Scientist. The idea is simple: students get to correspond with someone who actually does science, ask questions, and find out that researchers are people too. It's one of the more personally satisfying things I've signed up to do.
